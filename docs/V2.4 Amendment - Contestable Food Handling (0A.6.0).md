# V2.4 Amendment — Contestable Food Handling

**Status:** ADOPTED (V2.4). Adds a NEW model, `0A.6.0`; it does **not** amend
`0A.1.0`–`0A.5.0`, which stay exactly as they were. Relative to Spec v4 and the
earlier amendments it makes eating a multi-tick, contestable process **for
`0A.6.0` only**.

> Eating food is no longer instantaneous. An organism must handle a food item
> for several consecutive ticks before receiving its energy. During that
> interval the food travels with the organism. Physical contact with another
> organism can dislodge the food.

This creates a valuable, mobile and contestable resource **without** adding a
steal, defend, attack, carry or share action — or any new input or output at
all. The core principle is unchanged: **we simulate capabilities and
constraints, not behaviours.** Possession, interruption, pursuit of a holder,
retreat while consuming and repeated competition over one item become
*possible*; none of them is programmed, named or rewarded anywhere.

## 1. Why this slice exists

In `0A.5.0` physical bodies create mechanical competition, but food is consumed
the instant an organism wins it. There is nothing to possess, nothing to
interrupt, nobody to follow and no reason to move while eating. V2.4 adds the
smallest physical resource process that makes those things possible.

## 2. The controller is untouched

`0A.6.0` has exactly the `0A.5.0` controller:

- the same ten V2.1 inputs and the same Elman recurrence (hidden width 8);
- the same four outputs — forward, turn, **eat**, reproduce;
- the same **188** neural parameters, the same morphology, the same mutation
  and founder draw schedules;
- the same physical bodies and the same collision resolver.

Deliberately NOT added: a grab, release, steal, hold, defend or carry output; a
handling, possession or touching input. The **existing `eat` output** drives
the whole process.

## 3. Meaning of `eatRequested` in `0A.6.0`

| Holding? | `eatRequested` | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| no | true | attempt to acquire an eligible free item |
| no | false | do nothing |
| yes | true | continue handling the held item |
| yes | false | **release it**, here and now, progress reset |

There is no separate release action: not asking to eat *is* letting go.

## 4. Handling contract

`handling.ticksRequired` = **5** (`handlingTicksRequired`). Five consecutive
successful handling ticks consume one item; acquisition is the first of them.

- **Acquisition.** An organism may acquire an item when it requested eat, the
  item is within the unchanged `food.feedingRange`, the item is free, and the
  organism is not already holding something. Competition is the EXISTING food
  competition, unchanged: among eligible organisms the **nearest** wins, exact
  distance ties go to the **lower organism id**, items are visited in ascending
  food id order, and an organism takes at most one item per tick. No new
  competition score, no size term, no ownership, no randomness.
- **One item per organism; one holder per item.** Holding state lives on the
  FOOD item (`holderId`, `handlingProgress`), so "one holder per item" is
  structural; "one item per organism" is an acquisition eligibility rule.
- **Progress.** Each maintained handling tick advances progress by exactly one:
  1 on acquisition, then 2, 3, 4, and consumed at 5.
- **Completion.** The item is removed from the world and the holder receives
  exactly the ordinary `energy.foodEnergyValue`, credited in the unchanged
  energy-gain phase with the unchanged cap semantics. There is no bonus for
  handling longer, no way to keep a finished item, and no inventory or storage
  of any kind.
- **Reset.** Progress returns to 0 on voluntary release, on dislodgement and on
  the holder's death. There is no partial-progress carry-over: a reacquired
  item starts again at 1.
- **Timing.** An organism that completes handling this tick is no longer
  holding anything, so it may acquire a free item in the same tick's
  acquisition step (that is what the documented order implies). It is still one
  consumption per organism per tick.

## 5. Food movement while held

After movement and body-overlap resolution, a held item's position becomes its
holder's resolved position. One consequence of the documented order is worth
stating: on the tick an item is **acquired**, positions are synced before
acquisition, so the item stays where it lay — within feeding range of its new
holder — and travels with the holder from the next tick onwards.

A held item remains a real world food item throughout. It is never duplicated,
never removed from the ecological food count, and it counts towards
`food.worldFoodCapacity` exactly like a free item — so carrying can never
create extra regeneration.

## 6. Food sensing while held

Food perception is **not** special-cased. A held item is part of the food world
and participates in the existing nearest-visible-food rules. Therefore other
organisms may see an item moving with its holder, and the holder sees its own
item at zero distance under the existing zero-distance convention
(`foodVisible` 1, `foodDistance` 0, `foodAngle` exactly 0). There is no "I am
carrying food" input, and the item is not hidden from anyone. This is the
simplest continuation of the existing sensing model.

## 7. Contest / dislodgement

> Physical contact with another organism breaks handling.

The contact set comes from the ACTIVE body resolution — the one that runs after
movement (phase 4b). `resolveBodyOverlap` reports the ids of organisms that
were overlapping **another organism** when that resolution began, i.e. the
bodies that actually met as a result of this tick's movement. It is taken from
the resolver's **first pass** only, deliberately: later passes correct the
solver's own corrections, so contact would otherwise depend on
`body.separationPasses`, a solver parameter, rather than on what the organisms
did. The report is derived, per-resolution information: it is never stored on
an organism, never persisted, never canonical and never sent to an observer.

If a holder is in that set, its item is dislodged: released at the holder's
resolved post-collision position, progress reset to 0.

- No damage, no energy transfer, no stun.
- **No organism receives the item.** There is no steal, no recipient and no
  event — the item simply becomes free again and can be competed for later.
- **A dislodged (or voluntarily released) item may NOT be reacquired in the
  same tick.** It becomes eligible again on the next one. This removes
  same-tick drop/regrab ordering questions entirely.
- **Multiple contacts do nothing extra.** Any genuine contact dislodges once
  and resets progress once. No size-based probability, no strength check, no
  grip, no random contest, no stochasticity of any kind.

**What does NOT count as a contest:** wall clamping, visual proximity,
vision-cone overlap, food proximity, and reproduction on its own. In
particular, **the post-birth passive separation (phase 17b) is NOT a contest
event.** Newborns did not act during their birth tick, and a parent must not
lose its food merely because a child spawned next to it. This is tested
directly.

**On the floating-point floor.** The overlap test is the physics' own strict
`centreDistance < rA + rB`, with no second threshold and no epsilon. Bodies
separated to tangency on a previous tick can still register as touching by a
few parts in 10¹⁵, and that counts as contact. That is deliberate: one
definition of contact, no tuning knob. Crowded organisms therefore find it
harder to finish handling, which is an ecological consequence to observe, not a
bug to patch.

## 8. Handling and size

No size-based food rule is added. Size already matters indirectly: a larger
body resists displacement more (V2.3), and contact disrupts handling. There is
no size-based handling speed, food retention, bite strength, grip strength or
possession strength. Observe first.

## 9. Movement while handling

A holder moves and turns normally. It is not frozen, not slowed, not charged
extra, and gains no carrying mass or handling-specific metabolism. The only
costs and risks in this slice are the delayed energy gain, the need to keep
requesting eat, and the risk of physical interruption. "Move while consuming"
is therefore possible without any escape behaviour being programmed.

## 10. Death and reproduction

- **Death.** A holder that dies before completing releases its item at its
  final position with progress reset. The item stays in the world and **no food
  energy is granted** — dying is never a way to eat, and a death never removes
  food from the ecology. (An organism whose handling *completes* on its dying
  tick is credited normally and may be rescued by it, exactly as instantaneous
  feeding could always rescue.)
- **Reproduction.** Unchanged. A holder may reproduce if the existing rules
  allow it; the food stays with the parent. The child inherits neither the
  item, nor progress, nor any possession state, and its recurrent memory is
  still exactly zero. Because handling state is food-owned, a child cannot
  carry one structurally.

## 11. Food regeneration

Unchanged: the same three canonical draws per attempt, the same fertility
weighting, the same hard cap — counted over **all** existing items, held ones
included. No replacement item is ever spawned because something is being
handled.

## 12. Lifecycle timing — the final order

Sense → Decide → Resolve is preserved. Phases 1–5 and 9–20 are the §20.72 order
unchanged; V2.3's 4b/17b stay where they are; V2.4 replaces the instantaneous
phases 6–7 and adds one release step:

```
 1     Snapshot (S_t)
 2     Sense                       the authoritative pre-decision world
 3     Decide                      ActionIntent + the single recurrent memory
                                   advance, buffered and applied after all decide
 4     Movement resolution
 4b    Body overlap resolution     ACTIVE; its first-pass contact set is the
                                   contest set for this tick
 5     Movement energy expenditure
 6-7   FOOD HANDLING (0A.6.0):
         a. held items take their holder's resolved position
         b. drops: holders with eatRequested == false, and holders in the
            contact set; progress 0, item free at the holder's position,
            NOT reacquirable this tick
         c. progress += 1 for every item still held
         d. completion at ticksRequired: item removed, holder marked fed
         e. acquisition: eligible non-holders compete for the remaining FREE
            items under the unchanged competition semantics; progress 1
 8     Energy gain                 foodEnergyValue to whoever COMPLETED
 9-15  Reproduction eligibility, resolution, cost, child construction
 16    Death resolution            one combined pass
 16b   Dead holders release food   at the final position, progress 0, no energy
 17    Births/removals become active
 17b   Body overlap resolution     PASSIVE; deliberately NOT a contest
 18    Food regeneration           held items counted in the cap
 19    Telemetry (read-only)
 20    Advance tick
```

The externally observable contract: a new acquisition counts as progress 1;
handling needs 5 successful consecutive ticks; contact interrupts before
completion for that tick; dropped food cannot be reacquired until the next
tick; and handling uses post-movement, post-collision physical positions.

Collision and handling never reach the sensory vector already used this tick
(sensing reads S_t, which the tick never modifies — a deep-frozen S_t still
steps), never cause a second neural evaluation, and never advance memory again.

## 13. Persistent runtime state and canonical hashing

Handling is future-affecting, so it is canonical. Each food record of a
`0A.6.0` world carries `holderId` (an organism id, or `null`) and
`handlingProgress` (0 when free). Two worlds that differ only in who is holding
what, or in how far along they are, hash differently and have different
futures. Derived contact information is never stored.

Food records of every other model are exactly the historical `{ id, x, y }`, so
their canonical strings and hashes are untouched, and canonicalization refuses
a world whose food does not match its model.

## 14. Snapshot format v3

| Format | Models | Adds |
|---|---|---|
| v1 | `0A.1.0`, `0A.2.0`, `0A.3.0` | — (historical) |
| v2 | `0A.4.0`, `0A.5.0` | per-organism memory and recurrent weights |
| **v3** | `0A.6.0` | per-food `holderId` and `handlingProgress` |

Each model has exactly one format, checked against the model before anything
else is trusted. Validation rejects: missing `holderId`/`handlingProgress` on a
`0A.6.0` item; a non-integer or out-of-range progress (held items must be
`1 .. ticksRequired`, free items exactly 0); a holder id that is not a living
organism of that world; one organism holding two items; handling state on a
model that eats instantaneously; a v3 state relabelled `0A.5.0`; and a v2 state
relabelled `0A.6.0`. Nothing is migrated and no handling state is ever invented
for a historical model.

Exact resume is proved for a snapshot taken at every progress (1, 2, 3, 4 — 5
is unreachable in stored state, since an item that reaches it is consumed
inside the same step) and immediately after a dislodgement, in process and
across processes.

## 15. Observer / Observatory

Observer protocol stays **v1**. Frames carry no holder id, handling progress,
possession state, dislodgement or theft event, handling bar or debug icon — the
food entry is exactly `{ id, x, y }`. A held item is visible precisely because
its ordinary position moves with its holder. The frontend is unchanged, infers
no holder, and remains strictly read-only.

## 16. Explicitly not added

Grab/release/steal outputs, attack, damage, predation, health, bite, weapons,
inventory, long-term storage, nests, territory, ownership recognition, sharing,
food or energy transfer, signalling, communication, new sensory inputs,
possession/touch/handling inputs, new neural outputs, neural or memory
architecture changes, a movement penalty or carrying cost while holding,
size-based grip, probabilistic dislodgement, a food-defence rule, lineage or
parent recognition, species, mating choice, cooperation, chase/flee,
pathfinding, learning, RL, plasticity, ecology tuning, spatial indexing,
observer protocol v2, neural/handling UI, unrelated refactors.

## 17. Performance

Handling is a plain deterministic scan, O(food × organisms), with no spatial
index and no new engine. Measured on the development VM (linux-arm64, Node
22.23.2) at the standard 60-item food cap: 0.052 ms/tick at 25 organisms, 0.209
at 100, 0.589 at 250, 1.010 at 400. In real `0A.6.0` worlds (populations of
roughly 20–30) it is 0.045–0.10 ms of a 0.10–0.32 ms tick. No pathological
regression, so nothing was optimised.

## 18. Observed, not interpreted

At the canonical seed 20260910 the `0A.6.0` world has **no births at all and
dies out at tick 2,854** (the golden hash still fingerprints the whole
10,000-tick run; checkpoints at 500 / 1,000 / 2,000 cover it while alive).
Seeds 1 and 2 also die out; **seed 3** is the first of 1, 2, 3, … alive at tick
10,000, and **seed 8** is the most active world found (coverage seeds, clearly
not canonical). Contestable handling is plainly a much harsher ecology than
`0A.5.0`'s instantaneous feeding: energy arrives at best once per five ticks of
uninterrupted work, and contact is common. Extinction is a legitimate result —
no parameter was tuned and no seed was shopped to avoid it.

Mechanics measured over 6,000 ticks of the seed-8 world (classification exact:
each release checked against that tick's `eatRequested` and active contact
set): 6,649 acquisitions, **675 completions**, 4,426 voluntary releases (eat
went false), **1,537 dislodgements by body contact**, 6 drops on a holder's
death, body contact on 4,303 of 6,000 ticks, and food carried a total of 10,863
world units. No claim is made about stealing, defending, hoarding, cooperation,
pursuit, strategy or intelligence — only the mechanics above were measured.
