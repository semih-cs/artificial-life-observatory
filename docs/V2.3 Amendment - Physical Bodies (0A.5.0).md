# V2.3 Amendment — Physical Bodies

**Status:** ADOPTED (V2.3). Adds a NEW model, `0A.5.0`; it does **not** amend
`0A.1.0`, `0A.2.0`, `0A.3.0` or `0A.4.0`, which stay exactly as they were.
Relative to Spec v4 and the earlier amendments it gives organisms solid bodies
**for `0A.5.0` only**.

> Organisms now physically occupy space and can displace one another.

This is not combat, attack, predation or damage. There is no new neural input,
no new output, no push action and no new persistent state. Existing movement
simply begins to have physical consequences when bodies meet, and the core
principle is unchanged: **we simulate capabilities and constraints, not
behaviours.** If chasing, blocking, yielding or crowding ever appear, they must
emerge from movement, morphology and contact — nothing in the code names them.

## 1. Why this slice exists

After V2.2 organisms can see food, boundaries and each other, remember recent
internal history, move, eat and reproduce — but other organisms still had
almost no physical consequence: they could see one another and pass straight
through one another. That limited the usefulness of organism sensing, of
`organismRelativeSize`, of the morphology `size` gene and of recurrent memory.

V2.3 adds the first low-level physical coupling between organisms, and nothing
else.

## 2. The controller is untouched

`0A.5.0` has exactly the `0A.4.0` controller:

- the same ten V2.1 inputs (food ×3, boundary ×2, own energy,
  `organismVisible`, `organismDistance`, `organismAngle`,
  `organismRelativeSize`) — `organismRelativeSize` keeps its exact V2.1
  definition;
- the same Elman recurrence `h_t = tanh(W_in x_t + W_rec h_(t−1) + b)`, hidden
  width 8;
- the same four outputs and `ActionIntent` (forward, turn, eat, reproduce);
- the same **188** neural parameters and the same mutation settings.

Deliberately NOT added: a collision, touching, contact, pain or velocity
input; a push, attack or body-force output. The information an organism has
about others is still exactly what V2.1 gave it. What changed is that the
information may now *matter*: relative size can predict a physical outcome
that did not exist before.

## 3. Physical radius — the authoritative contract

The simulation owns the biological meaning of a body
(`simulation-core/src/biology/physicalBody.ts`):

    physicalRadiusFromSize(size, config)
      = config.body.radiusBase + config.body.radiusPerSize * size

with `radiusBase = 2.0` and `radiusPerSize = 2.2` world units
(`DEFAULT_PHYSICAL_BODY_CONFIG`). Over the §10.4 size range `[0.5, 1.5]` the
radius runs `[3.1, 5.3]`.

- It depends on the **inherited morphology size gene and these constants and
  nothing else**: no runtime adaptation, no hidden random variation, no
  lineage, energy or age term.
- `radiusPerSize > 0` is validated, so a larger `size` is always a strictly
  larger body.
- These constants are the mapping the Observatory has drawn since Phase 0D
  slice 1 (`2.0 + 2.2 * size`), moved into the simulation as a documented
  contract, so the circle a viewer sees is the circle that collides. The
  renderer keeps its own copy of the two constants (the frontend never imports
  simulation types) and a test pins the two together. No world looks different
  from before.
- `config.body` is present on a physical-bodies model and **absent** on every
  other one — exactly as recurrent weights are — so the configuration, and the
  `configHash`, of `0A.1.0`–`0A.4.0` are byte-identical to what they were.
  `validateConfig` enforces both directions.

## 4. Overlap

Two **living** organisms overlap iff

    centreDistance < radiusA + radiusB

strictly. Exact tangency (`centreDistance == radiusA + radiusB`) is contact,
not overlap, and is never resolved. The test is done on squared distances, so
deciding it needs no square root and no transcendental function.

When an overlap occurs it is resolved by **spatial displacement only**. There
is no damage, no energy transfer, no attack event, no stun, no bounce
velocity, no momentum, no health and no collision-specific neural event. Only
`x` and `y` change.

## 5. Size-based displacement — the exact weighting

[LOCKED for `0A.5.0`] *Both organisms may be displaced, but the larger
organism must move less than the smaller one.*

For an overlapping pair with penetration depth
`p = radiusA + radiusB − centreDistance` and unit vector `n` from A to B:

    shareA = sizeB / (sizeA + sizeB)
    shareB = sizeA / (sizeA + sizeB)

    A moves by  −n · p · shareA
    B moves by  +n · p · shareB

Each body's share of the separation is the **other's** fraction of the
combined size. So:

- equal sizes share the work exactly 0.5 / 0.5;
- the ratio is continuous and monotone in size — there is no threshold, no
  "2× bigger always wins", no immovable body, and a small organism can always
  displace a large one, just less;
- size is not turned into a strength score and no strength or armour gene
  exists.

This is the **only** advantage size gains in V2.3. Its existing energetic cost
(movement cost scales with size) is unchanged and deliberately not rebalanced.
The new trade-off — a larger body costs more, occupies more space and is
harder to displace — is left for observation, not tuning.

## 6. The resolver

`resolveBodyOverlap(organisms, world, config)` performs a fixed number of
deterministic **Jacobi** passes (`body.separationPasses`, default 4):

1. take the living organisms in **ascending id order** — this ordering, not
   the caller's array order, is what everything else uses (floating-point
   addition is not associative, so a fixed accumulation order is required, not
   merely tidy);
2. for every pair `i < j` in that order, measured against the positions as
   they were at the **start of the pass**, test overlap strictly and
   accumulate each body's correction; no organism is moved during the scan, so
   no pair sees another pair's in-progress correction and no pair has priority
   over another;
3. apply every accumulated correction at once, clamping each organism into the
   world rectangle with exactly the same hard-wall rule movement resolution
   uses; an organism with a zero correction is not written at all;
4. repeat; a pass that finds no overlapping pair ends the resolution
   immediately, so a world with no contact is left bit-for-bit untouched.

Properties, all under test: deterministic, RNG-free (no `Math.random`, no
BootstrapRNG, no world RNG), independent of input array ordering, stable under
id ordering, no iteration-order priority, bigger bodies move less, equal bodies
share equally, no new persistent velocity/physics state, no dependence on the
renderer, frame rate or observer connection.

It is a small simulation-core resolver, not a physics engine: no Matter.js, no
Box2D, no broad phase, no spatial index, no quadtree.

**Exact same centre.** If two centres are exactly identical the geometry gives
no direction. The direction is then taken from **pair identity alone**:
`(lowerId + higherId) mod 4` selects one of four axis-aligned unit vectors —
`(+1,0), (0,+1), (−1,0), (0,−1)` — pointing from the lower-id organism to the
higher-id one. Axis-aligned vectors are exactly representable in binary
floating point, so this adds no trigonometry and no platform-sensitive
arithmetic, and it draws no random number from any stream.

**When complete separation is impossible.** Dense clusters, and bodies pressed
against a wall, can be geometrically impossible to separate. That is handled
honestly rather than hidden: the fixed pass budget runs out, any residual
overlap simply remains, and nothing random, timing-based or convergence-based
is introduced. The budget is part of the model, so the outcome is exactly
reproducible. Two residual regimes are documented and tested:

- a configuration the budget **can** resolve settles at the floating-point
  floor — exact tangency is not representable in binary floating point, so a
  penetration of the order of machine epsilon can survive the strict `<` test
  (measured ~4 × 10⁻¹⁵ world units against radii of ~4.2);
- a configuration it **cannot** (for example twelve bodies of radius 3.1–5.3
  on a 2.5-unit grid) keeps a bounded residual — far smaller than a body,
  strictly better than where it started, and identical on every run.

In a real seed-8 `0A.5.0` world at ~240 organisms the worst residual
interpenetration observed is under 10⁻³ world units and no pair is ever deeply
interpenetrating, against `0A.4.0`'s ~8.0 world units (essentially co-located)
at the same tick.

## 7. Lifecycle timing

**Sense → Decide → Resolve** is preserved exactly. Organisms still sense the
authoritative pre-decision state S_t, decide from it, advance recurrent memory
exactly once, and submit the same movement/action intentions. Collision
resolution belongs entirely to Resolve, and `stepWorld` inserts two
position-only steps into the §20.72 order and changes nothing else:

```
  4   Movement resolution
  4b  BODY OVERLAP RESOLUTION      (0A.5.0 only)
  5   Movement energy expenditure
  6-7 Feeding & food competition   ← uses post-collision positions
  ...
 17   Births/removals become active
 17b  BODY OVERLAP RESOLUTION      (0A.5.0 only) — newborns included
```

(Phase 4b runs immediately after the movement + energy loop, before feeding.)

The intended behavioural consequence: **being physically displaced can move an
organism into or out of feeding range.** An organism that was next to food and
is pushed away does not get the food. That is intended, and there is no "food
defence" rule of any kind.

What collision must NOT do, and does not:

- it never influences the sensory vector already used for this tick (sensing
  reads S_t, which the tick never modifies — a deep-frozen S_t still steps);
- no organism reacts neurally to another's already-resolved movement in the
  same tick;
- it causes no second neural evaluation.

## 8. Recurrent memory

V2.2 semantics are exact and unchanged. Collision creates no special memory
update. Hidden state advances once per acting tick, from the sensory decision
evaluation, is never recalculated after collision, is not inherited at birth,
and is persisted exactly as before. A pushed organism does not get an extra
decision.

## 9. Boundaries

The world boundary model is unchanged: hard walls, positions clamped into the
rectangle, no bouncing, no wall damage, no boundary-sensing change. Collision
displacement uses the same clamp as movement resolution, so it can never
produce a non-finite or out-of-world position. Where clamping absorbs part of
a body's displacement — a pair pressed into a wall — the pair may stay in
contact; this is deterministic and is the only situation in which the
larger-moves-less ordering can be broken (measured: 15 of 4,688 real
contacting pairs in a seed-8 world, every one of them at a wall).

## 10. Reproduction and newborn overlap

Offspring placement is **unchanged**: the same polar offset from the parent,
the same two canonical RNG draws, in the same order. A newborn can therefore
land inside its parent or a neighbour. Rather than inventing a birth-time free
space search, a reproduction failure or a parent-specific rule, the **same
passive body-separation rule** is applied once to the post-birth living
population (phase 17b).

- the newborn receives **no** extra neural action;
- its recurrent hidden state is still exactly zero;
- **no extra RNG draw** is made — the canonical draw schedule for a birth is
  identical to `0A.4.0`'s, and the child's genome is bit-identical to the one
  `0A.4.0` would produce from the same state (both tested);
- no lineage-specific and no "parent protection" rule exists;
- physical separation affects positions only — never genomes, never memory.

## 11. Feeding

Semantically unchanged except that feeding sees post-collision positions.
Feeding range, food energy value, the food competition rule (nearest eligible
eater wins, exact ties by ascending organism id), the one-food-per-organism
per-tick rule, the eat threshold, food regeneration, handling — none of them
changed.

## 12. Persistence and hashing

Physical bodies add **no future-affecting per-organism state**: a body is a
pure function of `morphology.size`, and displacement changes only `x` and `y`.
Position, morphology and the existing recurrent memory are all already stored,
so:

- `0A.5.0` uses **snapshot format v2**, unchanged. There is **no format v3**.
- Format v1 remains exactly as it was for `0A.1.0`–`0A.3.0`; the `v1.0.0` and
  V2.1 fixtures still load byte-exactly.
- Nothing is migrated. A `0A.4.0` world is never relabelled `0A.5.0` (its
  configuration would have to gain a `body` section, which `validateConfig`
  refuses) and the reverse is refused too. A recovered world always uses its
  stored model.
- No collision metadata is added to snapshots or canonical state: the stored
  organism record is exactly the v2 record, and the serialized state contains
  no radius, overlap, contact, collision, displacement, damage or health
  field.
- Canonical serialization is unchanged; positions already affect the canonical
  state, so the physics is fingerprinted through them. The four historical
  golden hashes are unchanged; `0A.5.0` has its own.
- `0A.5.0` save → load → resume is exact (tested at four resume points, in
  process and across processes, and live through SIGINT and recovery).

## 13. Observer / Observatory

Observer protocol stays **v1**; the frame shape is unchanged and carries no
collision event, contact flag, push indicator, force arrow or physics debug
data — and still no memory, weights, inputs or intents. The Observatory simply
displays organism positions as before. The only frontend change is that the
existing body-radius helper now states, and is tested against, the simulation's
radius contract; the drawn radius is numerically identical to what it has
always been. The frontend remains strictly read-only.

## 14. Performance

The plain deterministic O(N²) pair scan, as the organism sensing scan already
is. No spatial hash, quadtree, broad-phase engine, GPU collision or worker
system was added. Measured on the development VM (linux-arm64, Node 22.23.2),
seed 8:

| Population | Full `stepWorld` | One resolver call | Two calls per tick |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 | 0.125 ms | 0.0016 ms | 2.5% of the tick |
| 113 | 1.13 ms | 0.079 ms | 13.9% |
| 243 | 3.68 ms | 0.356 ms | 19.3% |
| 234 | 3.41 ms | 0.336 ms | 19.7% |

No pathological regression, so nothing was optimised.

## 15. Explicitly not added

Attack, damage, health, predation, cannibalism, stealing, food carrying,
grab/release, handling time, food ownership, territory, explicit blocking,
push action, strength gene, armour gene, pain input, touching input, collision
input, new sensory channels, new neural outputs, signalling, communication,
energy transfer, mating choice, species, kin/lineage/parent recognition,
flocking, chase/flee logic, collision-avoidance rules, pathfinding, learning,
RL, plasticity, neural architecture changes, memory changes, ecology tuning,
spatial indexing, neural observability UI, observer protocol v2, snapshot
format v3, unrelated refactors.

## 16. Observed, not interpreted

At the canonical seed 20260910 the `0A.5.0` world has **two births and dies
out at tick 2,551** (the golden hash still fingerprints the whole 10,000-tick
run; checkpoints at 500 / 1,000 / 2,000 cover it while alive). Seeds 1–7 also
die out; seed 8 — the first of 1, 2, 3, … alive at tick 10,000, the same
living seed V2.2 used — reaches ≈ 234 organisms with 1,291 births.

Extinction is a legitimate result. No seed was shopped for a prettier
trajectory, no ecological parameter was touched, and none of this is evidence
that physical bodies help or hurt. These are single trajectories of a new
model.

What can be said about the physics, from measurement rather than impression:
organisms are in contact constantly (every one of 400 consecutive ticks in a
seed-8 world, ~19 contacting pairs per tick at population ~200); in 4,688 real
contacting pairs of unequal size the larger organism was displaced less in
99.7% of them, and every one of the 15 exceptions was a pair clamped against a
world wall; about 12% of displacement events happen within 15 world units of a
food item. No claim is made here about territory, dominance, cooperation,
aggression, strategy or intelligence.
